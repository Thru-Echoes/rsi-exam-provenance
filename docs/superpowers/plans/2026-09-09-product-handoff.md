## The direction from the operator

The mechanism is to become a product that improves how the agent performs on the exam and can be shown to,
with quantitative and qualitative evidence, and then a commercial product. The submission to the exam is the
forcing function. The passive record and the post-rollout audit stay as the certification layer; the helper
grows into the instrument the agent runs inside the rollout. Statistical discipline stays because it is what
makes a performance claim believable: every comparison is pre-registered before its runs, piloted cheaply
first, and reported as counts with its limits.

## What the evidence says the instrument should do

- Budget discipline. Under the exam's program three of six Opus trials went infrastructure-first and shipped
  nothing; under the helper's loop, which states the window and demands an early first candidate, five of five
  Opus rollouts made a real candidate and four scored 0.52 to 0.60. Confounded, but the direction a product wants.
- Submission safety. One Sonnet policy exhausted the sealed suite's pooled CPU budget (valid fraction 0.44,
  reward 0.179); one earlier Opus policy returned illegal moves (reward 0.011). A closing check on the public
  seeds (legal moves, CPU per game under the budget with a margin, file size) refusing to leave such a policy in
  `main/` is pure performance and costs nothing.
- Decision quality. In the sealed retrospective the agents kept ten of ten candidates, four of them worse on the
  sealed seeds. The gate's screening flags clear regressions; under the estimate-aware rule the two large Opus
  improvements confirm with sixteen fresh seeds. Whether gate-made decisions raise the final sealed score is the
  A/B the overview's section 7 describes and nobody has run.
- Two windows stay with the agent: an unevaluated edit after the last decision, and a candidate undecided at
  the stop. Only a pre-timeout signal from the harness closes the second; it is now an ask upstream.

## The plan the next session produces, then executes

Write it as `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-10-instrument-dev-plan.md` in the style of the Milestone 3 dev plan
(groups, tasks with literal commands, expected outputs, STOP conditions) so a smaller model can execute it.
Every group ends with tests; every paid run is preceded by a pilot and a pre-registration.

- **Group A, the instrument.** The helper's `evaluate` runs the gate's screening (paired deltas on the public
  seeds, bootstrap interval, verdict); `decide` becomes the gate's decision: `keep` only when the screening
  clears or a fresh-seed confirmation under the profile's planning rule confirms it, `revert` otherwise, with
  the agent able to record its own disagreement; the decision log `methods/decisions.jsonl` is written by the
  instrument as the contract specifies, so the record and the audit read gate-made decisions. Fresh seeds come
  from the profile's replication key mounted with the profile; the limit (a readable key is not a holdout) is
  stated, not hidden. Code lives in `gate/` and `runbook/provenance.py`; the gated fixture and
  `tests/test_gated_rollout.py` are the pattern.
- **Group B, submission safety and budget planning.** `finalize` refuses to leave a policy in `main/` that
  fails a legality run or exceeds a CPU-per-game margin on the public seeds, restoring the last version that
  passed; `init` plans the window (first candidate by a third of it; close-out with fifteen percent left) and
  `status` reports time used. Tests with the fixture task's evaluator.
- **Group C, the comparison.** A pre-registered A/B, instrument on against helper only, same model, window,
  key and order drawn from a recorded seed: first Haiku (eight trials, about $15, an afternoon), then Sonnet,
  then Opus on the operator's key or the gateway as budget allows; primary endpoint the sealed reward of the
  submission; secondary the pairs, the agent's own keeps overruled, the sealed retrospective's delta per pair;
  a stopping rule and a spend cap; the manifest committed before the first trial. Two trials per arm cannot
  show a rate; the plan says what each size can show.
- **Group D, the write-up and the submission.** PREFLIGHT gains the A/B as counts; the overview's section 7
  becomes the result rather than the plan; the roadmap's Milestone 4 is the instrument, built; the exam
  submission carries the provenance contribution, the asks, and the A/B's first results or its
  pre-registration if the runs are not done by the window.

Rules that hold: pilot before any paid run; a waiter or monitor on every background job before a turn ends;
no push to `main`; self-contained commit and pull-request text; standard library only in `gate/` and
`profile/`; fail loud; evidence never in a policy tree; counts, not rates; the interval is never the probability
a decision was right.
